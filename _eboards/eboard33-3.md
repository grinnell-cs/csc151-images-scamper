### Monday

What's the largest value that `(random 10)` can return?

> 9

Lists: How would you add five to each element of a list called `numbers`?

> `(map (l-s + 5) numbers)`

> `(map (o add1 add1 add1 add1 add1) numbers)`

> `(map (lambda (x) (+ x 1)) numbers)`

> `(let ([add5 (lambda (x) (+ x 1))]) (map add5 numbers))`

Characters: How do we determine the collating sequence of a character?
Collating sequence: "The number associated with each character".

> `char->integer`

Characters: How do we determine if a character is upper-case?

> `char-upper-case?`

Predicates: Why would we write `(and (integer? x) (odd? x))`
rather than `(and (odd? x) (integer? x))`?

> To make sure that we always get #t/#f. The second one will crash on
  non-integers.

### Wednesday

Lists: What should you get from `(map + (list 1 2 3) (list 4 4 4))`?

> `(list 5 6 7)`

Anonymous functions: Rewrite `(lambda (x) (string-append "X" x))`
with `l-s` or `r-s`.

> `(l-s string-apprend "X")`

Strings: How do you extract the character at index 5 from a string?

> `(string-ref str 5)`

Lists: Which of the big three should you use to select some of the elements
of a list, forming a new list.

> The big three `map`, `filter`, and `reduce`.

> `map` applies a function to each element of a list, building a new list 
  (of the same length).

> `filter` applies a predicate to each element of a list, building a new list
  from those for which the predicate holds. (**The answer.**)

> `reduce` takes a two-parameter procedure and a list and reduces the list to
  a single value by applying the procedure repeatedly to neighboring elements.

Misc: Why shouldn't you write `(5)`?

> In Scheme, parentheses usually mean "apply a procedure", with the procedure
  appearing immediately after the open paren. 5 is not a procedure.
